Ward 5: 311 reporting, adjusted for growth

Ward 5 in northeast Calgary has grown faster than the 2021 Census can show. That made the study's per-resident figures unreliable for its fastest-growing communities. This page explains the problem, the model used to fix it, and the final 311 results for every Ward 5 community.

1 · The gap

Why growth made some figures unreliable

The study measures reporting per resident: 311 requests divided by population. The latest population count by community is the 2021 Census, and The City stopped its own yearly census after 2019. Cornerstone and Cityscape now have about 2.5 and 2.0 times as many residents as in 2021, so dividing today's requests by 2021 population made them look as if they report far more than they do: Cornerstone appeared to report 222% more than similar communities. Redstone, Saddle Ridge and Skyview Ranch grew more modestly, by 19% to 26%.

2 · The model

How the gap is closed

Population follows the number of homes people live in. The City's property assessment roll records every home and the year it was built, so it can rebuild each community's population month by month.

  1. Count homes. Every property on the 2026 assessment roll that is a dwelling (residential, with a building, residential use). Empty lots, parking stalls and storage units are left out.
  2. Allow time to move in. A home is counted as lived in one year after the year of construction on the roll, because the roll records homes from the year building starts.
  3. Count every unit in rental buildings. The roll lists a rental building as one property however many units it has. Units come from the building permit where one matches the property (43,700 units in 658 properties); people move in six months after the permit is completed. For older buildings without a permit, units are estimated from assessed value (39,100 units in 1,740 properties) and scaled down wherever they would exceed 2021 Census households. Each rental unit holds 2.0 people, or the community's average 2021 household size where that is smaller.
  4. People per home. Each community keeps its own people-per-home from the 2021 Census: 2021 population, less people in rental units, divided by other homes lived in on Census day. In Cornerstone that is 3.6.
  5. Population during the study. People per home × homes lived in, averaged over the 24 study months (October 2024 to September 2026).

Tested against The City's own counts

The same method was used to predict The City's 2019 civic census from its 2016 count. For the 15 communities that grew fastest, the typical error was 6%, against 30% if population is held at the earlier count. Across all 179 communities it was 3%; the average error for the fastest-growing was 13%.

Range

Counting homes as lived in half a year or a year and a half after construction, and 1.5 to 2.6 people per rental unit, gives the range. Cornerstone: 14,900 to 16,700 residents, 42.2 to 47.4 reports per 1,000. The conclusions don't change within the range.

Calgary as a whole

The model puts Calgary at about 1,430,400 people during the study, against about 1,571,000 in The City's estimate for October 2025, about 9% lower. The rest is likely more people per home than in 2021, new basement and backyard suites, and rental buildings not yet on the roll. The shortfall is spread across the city, so it has little effect on comparisons between communities. For Calgary's population, use The City's figure.

Births and deaths

Adding The City's births and deaths since 2021 (about 16,500 and 8,650 a year), shared out by each community's children and seniors, raises Ward 5 by about 4% and leaves the main finding at about 20%. It is kept as a check, not part of the model.

With the adjusted population, the study's main model was re-run for all 195 communities. Every figure on this page comes from that re-run.

What stands out

Taradale and Martindale report least

They report 32% and 26% less than similar communities, and have the ward's highest shares of residents with no English (9.1% and 9.5%). They are the natural place to start a local 311 sign pilot.

Cornerstone is still building

After adjusting for growth, Cornerstone reports +52% against similar communities, down from +222%. Much of the rest is construction and edge-of-city requests, such as 234 dead-animal pick-ups. Counting only defects (potholes, streetlights, sidewalks, sewers, trees), it reports 24.1 per 1,000, close to Martindale (25.2).

The ward reports least of all 14

Ward 5 has the fewest infrastructure reports per resident of any ward. Some of that is expected, because many of its communities are new. Compared with similar communities, the ward as a whole is 8% below.

The main finding holds

Re-running the whole study with adjusted population, a community where 5% of residents don't speak English reports about 19% less than one where 1% don't (95% range 6% to 31%), close to the original 23%.

Limits

  • Units in older rental buildings without a matching permit are estimated from assessed value. Rental buildings still under construction or leasing up may not be on the roll yet.
  • People per home is held at each community's 2021 level, and basement and backyard suites added since 2021 are not counted, so the model's Calgary total is about 9% below The City's estimate.
  • New homes are assumed to fill about a year after construction starts; rental units with permits six months after completion.
  • The 2026 Census (community figures expected from 2027) and The City's civic census returning in 2028 will allow a direct check.
