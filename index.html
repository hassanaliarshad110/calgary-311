Who Calgary's 311 hears

Calgary's 311 system responds just as quickly in poorer neighbourhoods as in richer ones. But it hears far less from neighbourhoods where many residents don't speak English. In a system that fixes what people report, problems nobody reports can go unfixed.

What would help

  1. Measure who 311 hears. Publish reports per 1,000 residents by community and ward next to response time, and refresh this gap analysis every year.
  2. Remove the language barrier. Launch the multilingual 311 app first where the gaps are largest, add photo-first reporting (snap, pin, pick an icon, no typing), and accept reports by text or WhatsApp in the most-spoken local languages.
  3. Close the loop. Tell every reporter when the problem is fixed, with a photo where possible. People report again when they see results.
  4. Reward reporting with Fix-It Points. Verified, non-duplicate reports earn points for City recreation drop-in passes or transit day passes, capped each month so the program can't be gamed.
  5. Run a Community Care Challenge. Community associations in low-reporting areas earn small grants for growth in verified reports per resident, with a public leaderboard by ward.
  6. Recruit 311 Community Ambassadors. Train multilingual residents through settlement agencies, faith centres and community associations to help neighbours report and to lead seasonal walk-and-report audits.
  7. Bring in young people. School and youth-group "map your block" projects that report real issues, recognised with volunteer certificates.
  8. Check, don't wait. Send proactive inspections, including the camera-based road defect detection the City has already tested, to under-reporting communities first.
  9. Measure "fixed", not just "closed". Track how often a closed request is followed by a new one for the same issue.
  10. Pilot it. Test points, ambassadors and in-language reporting for 12 months in two or three high-gap wards, compared with similar communities, before going citywide.
