Ward 5 and 311: who reports, and who doesn't

Ward 5 is Calgary's largest ward and makes the fewest 311 infrastructure reports per resident. The video explains how much of that is expected, where the gap is (Taradale and Martindale), and why a lower number isn't automatically good news. Subtitles are built in.

Full details: Ward 5 page · Findings. Download the video (MP4, 10 MB). The ward diagram in the video is schematic, not a map.

Transcript

Calgary has one number for everything from potholes to broken streetlights: 311. But does every neighbourhood use it the same way?

Ward 5, in Calgary’s northeast, is the city’s largest ward. It’s home to about 134,000 people.

An independent study looked at more than 800,000 311 requests, across 195 communities, over two years.

Ward 5 makes the fewest infrastructure reports per resident of all 14 wards. About 37 per 1,000 residents a year, against 67 citywide.

Part of that is expected. Many Ward 5 communities are new, and new roads and pipes break less. But compared with similar communities, the ward still reports 8% less.

The gap is concentrated in two older communities. Taradale reports 32% less than similar communities, and Martindale 26% less. They also have the ward’s highest shares of residents who can’t hold a conversation in English.

The service itself is not the problem. Ward 5 requests are closed as fast as anywhere else. And residents who do report use the 311 app just as often as the rest of Calgary.

So a lower number isn’t automatically good news. It can mean problems are going unreported.
